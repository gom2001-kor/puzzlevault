Choose your first PuzzleVault game

Compare all ten PuzzleVault games by play style, first action, and a manageable first goal, then choose a calm puzzle or a short timed challenge.

Choose an action you enjoy: comparing clues, arranging pieces, remembering positions, calculating, or watching a physical merge. Then choose a small first goal. Clearing one line or understanding one guess is more useful than immediately chasing an unfamiliar high score.

Each game opens in your browser. Follow a table link, then choose a mode. “First goal” is a suggested learning task, not a new reward or an extra game rule.

If you want a calm deduction puzzle

For ten quiet minutes of deduction, choose NumVault's Free Play, then Easy. Its four distinct digits avoid repeated-digit complications. Green confirms a digit and position; yellow means the digit belongs elsewhere. State one thing you learned before entering another guess.

If arranging objects feels more appealing than code deduction, try SortStack in Relaxed. Keep an empty tube available and check whether the destination is empty or has a matching top ball. A completed tube contains four matching balls. The point of this first attempt is to understand legal moves, not to predict a minimum-length solution.

For another calm starting point, TileTurn's small Classic boards let you compare a whole cross before pressing. The TileTurn worked guide shows why turning on more individual tiles is not always the best next move.

If you want a short timed challenge

Suppose you want a brief score attempt to compare with a friend. QuickCalc's duel gives both attempts the same 30-second course through a shared link, with the same question order and answer positions. Finish your run and use its share control to send that course. The other person plays later; this is not a live match.

Read the arithmetic before tapping. A wrong answer costs three seconds and resets the streak, so repeated guesses can erase the advantage of quick fingers. Shared scores are self-reported rather than verified leaderboard entries. Agree whether practice replays are allowed. The QuickCalc accuracy guide includes a worked comparison of the points lost by breaking a streak.

Daily courses and level packs serve different purposes

Where offered, Daily uses a date-based shared setup that changes at midnight UTC. The local calendar may show a different date around that boundary. Replay rules vary: NumVault records one Daily attempt, while PatternPop and QuickCalc allow practice replays. Choose Free Play or another available mode when you want a different kind of run.

ColorFlow, PipeLink, and TileTurn use level packs. Select a pack and work through its levels; do not look for a Daily tab on every game. Also read the actual finish condition: ColorFlow needs every pair connected, with full coverage as an extra scoring goal; PipeLink needs the required sources and destinations connected, not every tile powered.

Find help and understand your saved progress

Scroll below a game's controls to its how-to section and expandable questions. For a board you can study step by step, open the matching strategy article in the blog. If controls surprise you, read their labels before retrying: a tap can rotate a GridSmash tray piece, while a drag places it.

Scores, settings, and supported progress stay in this browser, without automatic synchronization to other devices. Clearing site data can remove them. Sharing communicates a result or supported challenge; it does not back up every record. Choose one game, complete its first small task, then decide whether to repeat or try another puzzle.
